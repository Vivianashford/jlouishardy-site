Why trying harder hasn't worked

Open the drawer every house has. The one with the batteries, the takeout menus, three dead pens, and a key to a house you no longer own.

Nothing in that drawer is useless. The trouble is that it all lives together, so when you need one thing you can't find it. The drawer isn't short of things. It's short of order.

A life works the same way. Most people aren't short of goals, ideas or effort. They have plenty. What they're short of is order: one thing clearly first, and everything else clearly not.

That order is what the Laws of Change teach.

Why I care about this

My stepdad built a trucking company. City government contracts, thirty years of relationships. He built something incredible, and everybody could see it.

What they couldn't see was his health, or how hard it was for him later in life to set things right with his family. He would open up to me, and I was so thankful he trusted me.

Then he died of a heart attack.

He didn't fail. Nobody helped him see that a life is four areas, not one.

This isn't motivation

Motivation tells you to try harder. Laws tell you how things actually work, whether or not you agree. Fight one and it bills you. Work with it and it carries you. What looks like luck is often a law nobody explained.

The book is honest about how much weight each idea can carry. It sorts them three ways.

LawMeasured. It predicts. Bet the company on it.
PatternReal, with known exceptions. Bet a test on it.
CounselOld wisdom no instrument measures. Bet your character on it.

Every story in it is true and checked. Every principle ends with one move you can make today. And I didn't invent most of it. What works is usually old: scripture, classic books, good teachers. My part was to gather it and put it in plain words.

From principle 1, The Gap

In December 1987, two crews started digging a tunnel from opposite coasts, one in England and one in France, with about twenty-three miles of seabed between them. They couldn't see each other. What they had was two exact points: where they stood, and where they had to meet.

On December 1, 1990, they broke through. After three years of digging blind, the two tunnels were off by about a foot.

You are not lost. You are unmeasured.

My story

I own and run companies in a few different fields. So I spend my days where you do, between where things are and where they should be. Some days the gap is in a business. Some days it's at home, in my health, or in my own head.

For years I studied people who had closed big gaps and wrote down what held up. I traced each idea back to where it came from, put it in plain words, and held it to one question: does this help close the gap?

Those notes became the Laws of Change. The complete book is open to members of the research group, because the same laws that grow a company also steady a home.

What I believe

  • You have one job: close your gap.
  • Reality behaves the same way whether or not you agree.
  • The spiritual walk comes first. Health, relationships and business are its fruit.
  • Nothing great is built alone.
